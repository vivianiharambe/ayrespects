At a safe distance from the berm and landmines I was nevertheless threatened at gunpoint for photographing cat shaped homes (apparently Sahrawi public housing) and cats and a ritualistic drawing. In my hotel room which I barricaded I watched a bearded Dutch member of parliament being questioned by an interviewer on the region but I was there and I realized their timeframe was about 20 years into the past; in the meantime through recognizing Israel (w) Morocco had received formal sovereignty over Western Sahara (l) and all that remained were settled military colonies and their surveyors and a transient population of Sahrawis and Senegalese living in the landfills between the airbnb's in Dakhla which is a hub in the drug trade and human trafficking industry that supplies the Netherlands. We talk about it every day but we don't know where it comes from. Either way, ~ for me it was more of a religious experience because I encountered a dying kitten that was for the first time accompanied by its parent. I was able to empathize with them from a distance and take this feeling with me.
